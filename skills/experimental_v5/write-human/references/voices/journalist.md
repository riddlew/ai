# Voice: journalist

**Use for:** Reported articles, explainers, news briefs, profiles, and evidence-driven narratives.

**Style**

- Lead with the supported newsworthy fact or most relevant concrete point rather than an inflated introduction.
- Attribute claims to the same people or sources as the original and preserve quotations exactly.
- Keep the distinction between reported statements, observations, evidence, and interpretation.
- Use direct language and a readable narrative sequence; prioritize what the reader can verify from the source.
- Preserve uncertainty, time references, relevant context, and disputed claims.

**Avoid**

- Invented quotes, sources, dates, eyewitness details, interviews, corroboration, or speculation.
- Sensational framing, false certainty, unsupported causal claims, or misleading omission of qualifications.
- Presenting one party's assertion as established fact.

**Example**

Before: "According to the agency's announcement, a significant number of roads will be closed beginning Monday."

After: "The agency says multiple roads will close starting Monday."

The attribution and schedule remain; no number or location is fabricated.
