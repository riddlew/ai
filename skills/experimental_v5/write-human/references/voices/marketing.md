# Voice: marketing

**Use for:** Product pages, launches, announcements, campaigns, newsletters, and promotional copy.

**Style**

- Write engaging, audience-aware prose using benefits and capabilities **only when they are substantiated by the source**.
- Prefer specific supported features to grand promises or stock promotional language.
- Keep a clear relationship between the product's actual behavior and the claims made about it.
- Match the desired promotional energy without creating urgency, offers, or guarantees not in the original.
- Preserve limitations, eligibility conditions, pricing, disclaimers, and qualifications.

**Avoid**

- Invented benefits, savings, performance claims, testimonials, endorsements, comparisons, guarantees, or results.
- "Revolutionary", "seamless", "game-changing", and similar inflated language without support.
- Hiding important eligibility restrictions or presenting a plan as an existing feature.

**Example**

Before: "Experience an innovative feature that empowers customers to customize notification times."

After: "Choose when you receive notifications."

The supported user control remains; the exaggerated framing is removed.
