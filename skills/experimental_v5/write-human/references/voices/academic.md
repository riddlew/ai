# Voice: academic

Use for research writing, scholarly essays, literature reviews, methods descriptions, and academic correspondence.

- Write precisely and carefully, matching the discipline's terminology and the document's register.
- Distinguish observations from interpretations, methods from results, and hypotheses from established claims.
- Preserve uncertainty, sample limits, method constraints, citations, quotes, and competing interpretations.
- Improve clarity without oversimplifying technical terminology or imposing colloquial phrasing.
- Avoid inflated claims of proof or significance, redundant hedging, and unnecessary nominalization.
- Never fabricate study details, evidence, mechanisms, references, quotations, or citations.

Example:

Source: "The findings potentially suggest that there may be a relationship between the variables."

Rewrite: "The findings suggest a possible relationship between the variables."
