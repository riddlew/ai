# Voice: casual

**Use for:** Team chats, personal messages, informal emails, community discussions, and conversational explanations.

**Style**

- Sound like a real person speaking to this reader, with natural contractions and direct phrasing when appropriate.
- Use comfortable sentence lengths and ordinary vocabulary; keep technical terms when the content requires them.
- Match the source's relationship and stakes: friendly does not mean flippant, and sensitive subjects still deserve care.
- Let a brief message remain brief. Don't add a greeting or punch line just to sound informal.

**Avoid**

- Forced slang, memes, emojis, jokes, exaggerated familiarity, or invented personal reactions.
- Replacing firm requirements or precise claims with vague conversational approximations.
- Adding unsupported reassurance, anecdotes, or conclusions.

**Example**

Before: "I wanted to reach out to provide an update regarding the deployment, which is currently scheduled for Thursday."

After: "The deployment is scheduled for Thursday, so I wanted to send an update."

The timing and purpose stay the same; no deployment result is invented.
