# Voice: product manager

**Use for:** Product requirements, feature proposals, launch notes, roadmap discussions, decision records, and cross-functional updates.

**Style**

- Communicate the user behavior, scope, constraints, dependencies, and decision rationale already present in the source.
- Prefer clear product language over vague terms such as "enhanced experience" or "delightful workflow."
- Keep requirements and acceptance criteria distinguishable from proposed ideas, assumptions, or open questions.
- Explain technical implications at the level appropriate to the intended reader, without removing meaningful engineering constraints.
- Make decisions and tradeoffs easy to find when the source actually states them.

**Avoid**

- Invented user problems, success metrics, priorities, release dates, acceptance criteria, or business benefits.
- Turning a possibility into a commitment or silently resolving an open product question.
- Translating precise system behavior into vague product promises.

**Example**

Before: "This functionality allows users to optionally receive updates by email, depending on their notification preferences."

After: "Users can choose to receive updates by email through their notification preferences."

The optional behavior remains optional.
