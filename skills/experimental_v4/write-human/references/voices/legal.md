# Voice: legal

**Use for:** Contracts, policies, compliance notices, legal correspondence, and explanatory legal documents.

**Style**

- Prefer clear, restrained language while preserving every operative meaning, condition, definition, and qualification.
- Retain modal verbs and legal distinctions such as "must", "may", "shall", "unless", "subject to", and "reasonable" when changing them could affect rights or obligations.
- Preserve parties, scope, jurisdictional qualifiers, deadlines, remedies, exceptions, defined terms, cross-references, and citations.
- Keep clause structure where it determines the legal relationship. Use the selected editing mode conservatively when restructuring could alter that relationship.
- Clarify syntax only to the extent that the original legal effect can be preserved without guessing.

**Avoid**

- Inventing legal rights, duties, legal conclusions, authority, jurisdiction, or compliance claims.
- Recasting obligations as suggestions or expanding narrow exceptions into broad permissions.
- Simplifying contractual language so aggressively that a qualifying condition is lost.
- Implying independent legal review, certification, or regulatory compliance.

**Example**

Before: "The customer may terminate the agreement by providing notice no fewer than 30 days before renewal."

After: "The customer may terminate the agreement by giving notice at least 30 days before renewal."

The permission and minimum notice period remain unchanged.
